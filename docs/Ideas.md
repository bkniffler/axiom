# Game Ideas (Axiom)

This repo already has a strong “feel” (starmap UI prototype) and a rich setting (`axiom/docs/Worldbuilding.md`). This document collects full game directions that match the world’s pillars and the “design-first” indie lessons in `axiom/docs/Indiegames.md`:

- **Systemic elegance**: few rules, deep combinations.
- **Perfect transparency (when it matters)**: choices feel “fair” and learnable.
- **Knowledge-based progression**: what changes is what the player understands.
- **Rule-breaking / meta**: the UI, the rules, and the player’s assumptions can become the puzzle.

---

## A. “Become A Real Game” From The Current UI (Expansion / Dilemmas)

### 1) Axiom: First Light
**Genre:** 4X‑lite / Grand Strategy with transparent forecasting  
**Pitch:** You play a new Progeny species starting on a single world, pushing from tribal origin → orbit → system control → sector influence inside the Axiometric Concordat’s political gravity.

**Core loop**
- Expand: scout → claim → connect (routes) → stabilize (governance).
- Decide: each milestone is a dilemma with a forecasted consequence window (short-term clarity, long-term uncertainty).
- Learn: “Axiom Current” tech starts as a predictive model; later it becomes a contested, dangerous capability.

**Signature dilemmas (worldbuilding-aligned)**
- **Concordat compliance vs. leverage:** adopt their anti‑AI doctrine, or build “too-helpful” modeling tools and risk Intercessor scrutiny.
- **Uplift vs. extract:** pre‑FTL encounters aren’t loot boxes; they’re moral and political liabilities that shape your legitimacy.
- **Progeny Union vs. Tri‑Council games:** build a fourth power bloc, or play Kahlbrax/Cygnan/Aethelgard against each other.

**Endings**
- Join the Concordat (stability), reshape it (union), or slip into the “cosmic layer” (Tzsari/Vertex/hidden physics).

### 2) Seed Sphere Exodus
**Genre:** Planet ecology builder → colony strategy  
**Pitch:** You’re “born” in an Ael’dari Seed Sphere. A sabotage (Wild Seers) breaks the carefully balanced trajectory, forcing early survival decisions that become your species’ ideology.

**Core loop**
- Keep a biosphere stable under chaos.
- Turn ecological mastery into tech (adaptations, symbioses, engineered castes).
- Export that identity off-world: colonies are “ecosystems with politics,” not generic outposts.

**Endings**
- Restore the intended trajectory, break it, or weaponize it.

### 3) Qu’lathi Frontier
**Genre:** Quarantine strategy / frontier management  
**Pitch:** Your expansion pushes toward (or around) the Qu’lathi Expanse—treated as a natural disaster, but possibly something else.

**Core loop**
- Build and maintain a living cordon: evacuation plans, supply routes, surveillance, containment fleets.
- Every “save” is also a potential spread vector.

**Endings**
- Earn legitimacy by holding the line, or learn what the Expanse actually is and face the consequences of knowing.

### 4) The Broken Chain
**Genre:** Liberation strategy / influence warfare  
**Pitch:** You begin under Vex’thra pressure (raids, subjugation, “incorporation”). Your empire grows by flipping systems through liberation, not conquest.

**Core loop**
- Identify leverage: trade, comms, leadership fractures.
- Run campaigns: aid resistance, sabotage extraction, counter‑propaganda.
- Live with second-order effects: the “freed” may choose Dra’viin harmony or Concordat “order” over your messy liberty.

**Endings**
- Become the heart of the Progeny Union, or the excuse the Tri‑Council uses to crack down.

## B. Deduction-First Directions (Move Away From “Expand The Galaxy”)

### 1) THE ANCHORED SEQUENCE
**Genre:** Knowledge-Based Mystery / Deductive Horror  
**Inspiration:** Outer Wilds × Return of the Obra Dinn × Inscryption  

**Pitch:** You are a junior analyst in the Concordat’s historical archives. Your job is simple: cross-reference recovered Precursor data fragments and flag inconsistencies. But the inconsistencies don’t add up. Dates that predate accepted history. Extinction events with no cause. References to entities inside stars.

The only thing that upgrades is what you know.

**Core loop**
- Explore a non-linear archive of recovered logs, coordinates, and artifact scans.
- Use a correlation engine (the game’s central UI) to link evidence across species, eras, and locations.
- When you identify a connection, the game unlocks a new piece of the archive you can now access (not gated by items—gated by understanding).
- Periodically, you’re asked to submit reports to your superiors; what you include vs. omit shapes narrative branches.

**Signature twist**
- The correlation engine is helpful. Too helpful. It subtly guides your conclusions.
- When you finally piece together evidence of the Tzsari, you realize the tool you’ve been using—the Anchored Sequence itself—has been steering you away from the truth for hours.
- The game’s UI is the antagonist.

**Win condition**
- There isn’t a single “win.” There are endings based on what you choose to do with forbidden knowledge: bury it, leak it, or try to act on it. Each ending recontextualizes the playthrough.

### 2) Axiom Intercessors: Case Files
**Genre:** Episodic investigation / anomaly forensics  
**Pitch:** You’re an Intercessor cell investigating “Harmonic Coefficient Deviations” across multiple worlds—each case is a contained deductive puzzle that also feeds a meta-mystery.

**Core loop**
- Arrive → interview (multi-species perspectives) → scan (tech/physics anomalies) → reconstruct timeline.
- File a conclusion that drives consequences: quarantine, sanction, containment, cover-up.

**Signature twist**
- The “right” answer is sometimes politically impossible, and the game tracks what you *chose to be true*.

### 3) The Vertex Atlas
**Genre:** Spatial deduction / topology puzzle  
**Pitch:** You’re mapping the Axiom Vertex’s impossible interior. Corridors loop, distances lie, and doors open based on *conceptual adjacency* rather than meters.

**Core loop**
- Build your own atlas: a player-made graph of rooms/constraints/rules.
- Test hypotheses: “If two chambers share a harmonic signature, they’re adjacent in folded space.”
- Progress by understanding folding rules, not by finding keys.

**Signature twist**
- Your atlas becomes a weapon: later, you can intentionally “mis-map” reality to access sealed layers (at a cost).

### 4) The Progeny Riddle (Origin Auditor)
**Genre:** Xeno‑genealogy deduction  
**Pitch:** Concordat science says Progeny species are unrelated. You’re tasked with proving or disproving the “Demiurgos / Genewright” myths using fragmentary biological, linguistic, and archaeological evidence.

**Core loop**
- Compare traits, myths, and genetic markers across species dossiers.
- Identify shared “design signatures” that point to common seeding events.
- Decide what counts as proof in a political system that *doesn’t want an answer*.

### 5) Silent Worlds Ledger
**Genre:** Systems-level detective story  
**Pitch:** Worlds go dark. Not exploded—just silent. You’re a logistics auditor tracing missing ships, vanished comms, and erased registry entries across decades.

**Core loop**
- Work backwards from shipping manifests, comms timestamps, and insurance claims.
- Spot impossibilities (mass moved without energy, jumps without routes, identical handwriting across centuries).
- Unmask whether it’s Dra’viin assimilation, Qu’lathi seepage, or something older.

### 6) Language Of The Current
**Genre:** Communication puzzle / cryptanalysis  
**Pitch:** In Axiom Current regions, entanglement comms carry “sideband” information. You decode signals that don’t behave like language—more like probability distributions.

**Core loop**
- Build a dictionary of patterns by experimentation (no tutorial solutions; the player learns the “physics grammar”).
- Use decoded messages to locate hidden sites and avert disasters.

**Signature twist**
- Some messages are not meant for you; decoding them changes what future messages can be.

### 7) The Obra‑Dinn‑Style Derelict (Choose A Setting)
**Genre:** Closed‑space identity deduction  
**Pitch:** A derelict habitat/fleet segment is found near a gravitationally extreme ruin. You reconstruct who everyone was and what happened, using partial sensor logs, personal artifacts, and “impossible” physics signatures.

**Setting options**
- A Nomyr flotilla segment that shouldn’t be able to anchor.
- A Chrysalis Station where Ix’thaum factions turned modification into warfare.
- A Seed Sphere where the sabotage isn’t just ecological.

### 8) The Anchored Sequence: “Baba Is You” Variant (Rule Editing UI)
**Genre:** Rule‑logic puzzle / meta mystery  
**Pitch:** The archive UI exposes its own inference rules (as manipulable blocks): `IF artifact.signature MATCHES X THEN classify AS Y`. You “solve” by rewriting the system’s logic—carefully—without triggering Intercessor containment protocols.

**Core loop**
- Modify inference rules to see new links.
- Each change has side effects: you can reveal truths, but also create blind spots and false trails that persist.

## C. Quick Filters (Pick One Direction Fast)

- If you want **the current starmap UI to be the core screen**: start with **First Light** (strategy) or **The Anchored Sequence / Vertex Atlas** (deduction).
- If you want **short sessions + strong replay**: **Intercessors: Case Files** or **Qu’lathi Frontier**.
- If you want **a single, iconic indie “hook”**: **UI as antagonist** (Anchored Sequence) or **topology as puzzle** (Vertex Atlas).

## D. Deckbuilding Directions

### 1) INTERCESSOR: WARRANT & RELIC
**Genre:** Roguelike tactical card battler  
**One-line identity:** A readable tactical card battler where legality is a resource, illegality is a strategy, and bureaucracy is an enemy that shows up mid-fight.

**Pitch:** You play as an Axiom Intercessor cell handling anomalous incidents for the Axiometric Concordat—empowered, semi-autonomous… and constantly monitored. Your “mana curve” is political authority, your strongest plays are illegal physics interventions, and the game pushes back mid-fight via bureaucratic audits.

#### Game System Summary

##### 1) Core Loop

**Run flow (Slay-the-Spire style map)**  
You travel node-to-node across a 3-Act assignment arc:
- Act 1: Containment Incidents (learn systems; audits mild)
- Act 2: Deep Anomalies (weirder site rules; audits become specific)
- Act 3: Existential Casework (audits become “mid-combat bosses”)

**Node types**
- Case (Combat): enemy deck + Site + objective
- Briefing (Event): choose approach; boons/curses; gain intel
- Requisition Office (Shop): buy/remove cards; buy “Permits”
- Evidence Locker (Upgrade): upgrade by “documenting” it
- Relic Vault (Risk Shop): powerful relics but increases monitoring
- Lurking (Elite Event): big reward, triggers an Audit immediately
- Act Boss: Major Incident: multi-phase case with unique audit rules

##### 2) Combat Layer

**The Site Board (tactical but readable)**  
A small positional board: 3 lanes × 3 depth
- Lanes: Left / Center / Right
- Depth: Entry (you) → Corridor → Core (enemy/objective)

Units spawn on their side and can Advance (some can Retreat/Jump/Teleport).

**Site tiles / lane rules (environment as puzzles)**  
Examples:
- Sealed Door: can’t Advance until opened
- Void Corridor: entering takes damage but grants “Phase”
- Unstable Geometry: swaps random occupied tiles end of round
- Containment Field: reduces Coefficient gain in lane but nerfs relics
- Breach Node: spawns extra enemy tokens unless Sealed

##### 3) Combat Resources

**1) Authority (your normal “mana”)**
- Gain Authority each turn up to Jurisdiction Cap
- Pays for most Agents and Directives
- “Legal” play patterns improve efficiency (refunds, discounts, next-cost-0)

**2) Override (illegal spending)**
- Lets you do Relics “off-procedure”
- Break board rules (teleport/rewind/force outcomes)
- Ignore costs or cast a Directive twice, etc.
- Generated by risk-taking (self-damage/sacrifice), black channels, relic friction, or “borrow from future Authority”
- Spending Override creates consequences (mostly Audit pressure)

**3) Coefficient (reality strain)**
- Generated by Relics and “physics crime” tools
- Crossing thresholds triggers Deviations (site warps / anomalies)
- Deviations are designed as danger + opportunity
- You can build around triggering Deviations intentionally

##### 4) The Signature Twist: Audits

**Audit Meter**  
Fills when you:
- Spend Override
- Spike Coefficient / violate procedure

When it hits threshold, an Audit triggers mid-fight (not after).

**Audit events (interactive constraints, not random death)**  
When an Audit triggers, you get a finding (choice-based or semi-random with counterplay), such as:
- Seizure Order: lock a Relic for 3 turns OR discard it for Authority
- Procedural Freeze: Jurisdiction Cap -2 next turns OR play Compliance cards
- Censure Notice: choose card type you can’t play next turn
- Chain-of-Custody Review: add Paperwork; Relics cost more while it remains
- Field Inspector: neutral unit appears; protect it to reduce Audit; if it dies, Audit spikes

**Paperwork (status cards with gameplay)**  
Paperwork is a “bureaucracy resource”: can be dead weight, but also spendable and build-enabling.

Examples:
- Form 27B (0): Exhaust → reduce Audit by 1, draw 1
- Containment Log (1): gain Block; upgraded also reduces Coefficient
- Censure Appeal (2): remove Censure; if you played a Relic, gain Override

##### 5) Cards & Deck Structure

Your deck is split into “legal tools” and “physics crime.”

**Card types**
- Agents (units): persist on the board, block lanes, escort objectives, hold tiles; can generate Authority/Override or stabilize Coefficient
- Directives (spells/rules): clean, readable actions (move, seal, censure, warrants, orders, buffs)
- Relics (big unfair effects + consequences): strongest plays; spike Coefficient; have Charges and Dispensation conditions (legal use reduces penalties)

##### 6) Keywords & Rules Language

Designed to read like Hearthstone-style keywords.

**Procedure / authority keywords**
- Jurisdiction Cap: max Authority per turn
- Override: illegal spending that increases Audit pressure
- Audit: mid-fight crackdown event
- Censure: disable a card type/lane/unit ability for 1 turn
- Seal: disable a hazard/spawner until broken
- Dispensation: “legal relic use” condition reducing penalties
- Chain of Custody: relics safer if documentation maintained

**Physics / anomaly keywords**
- Coefficient X: gain X Coefficient
- Deviation: triggers when Coefficient crosses thresholds
- Stabilize X: reduce Coefficient by X (often also reduces Audit a bit)

##### 7) Cases: More Than “Kill Everything”

Each Case is defined by:
- Enemy deck identity (what it keeps doing)
- Site rule (board modifier)
- Objective (often not lethal)

**Objective examples**
- Secure the Artifact: hold Core tile 3 turns
- Extract the Warden: move Core → Entry
- Seal the Breach: apply 3 Seals before timer ends
- Neutralize the Fork: kill a replicator before it copies 5 times
- Survive Containment: endure waves until turn 8

**Enemy deck examples**
- Rogue Syth’aar Fork: replicating tokens; learns from your Directives
- Living Ruin: the Site attacks; enemies “immortal unless Documented”
- Qu’lathi Tendril Incursion: spores infect tiles; dead units become enemies
- Cult Cell: rituals randomize lanes/costs; disrupt stability

##### 8) Build Archetypes (Draft Identities)

- Compliance Control: low Override; wins via Seal/Censure/lane locks; few Audits
- Overreach Combo: generate Override; illegal Relics; survive stacked Audits
- Containment Midrange: beefy Agents + door control; great for escort/hold
- Evidence Engine: “document” targets; convert Evidence into value + safe relic usage
- Deviation Alchemist: intentionally hit Coefficient thresholds for benefits; high skill/variance

##### 9) Boss Philosophy

Bosses “weaponize procedure,” not just HP.

Examples:
- Act 1: Failure: hazards escalate; lower audit threshold tutorial
- Act 2: The Hearing: the boss is an audit fight; must endure constraints; win by Evidence/Compliance
- Act 3: Anchored Intervention: “help” (Anchored Guidance buffs) that adds permanent constraints mid-fight—power traded for future collapse

##### 10) Crew System (1–3 Intercessors)

You run a crew of 1–3 characters, each with strong pros/cons that shape the run.

Each Intercessor provides:
- Passive Doctrine (Pro): always-on enabling rule
- Liability Clause (Con): always-on drawback (real cost)
- Signature Card: starts in deck
- Once-per-Case Command: a strong “button” (0-cost Exhaust)

Crew-size tradeoffs:
- More crew = more power and flexibility, but more oversight exposure
- Coordination Tax: start each Case with extra “Briefing Minutes” Paperwork  
  - 1 crew: +0, 2 crew: +1, 3 crew: +2
- Exposure scaling: extra crew makes Override/Coefficient fill Audit faster

Lead assignment:
- Before each Case, pick a Lead crew member for a small start-of-fight boon (keeps crew tactical without micromanagement).

##### 11) Between-Cases Management: After-Action + Assignments

A lightweight meta layer to add politics, trading, and casework without turning into a 4X.

**A) After-Action Disposition (choose 1 after each Case)**
- Filed Clean: gain Influence; reduce Oversight; weaker loot
- Redacted: balanced loot; moderate Oversight
- Unreported: best loot / illegal offers; Oversight spikes; next Case starts with higher Audit pressure

**B) Downtime Assignments (each crew member chooses one)**  
(So 1–3 crew also matters outside combat.)
- File & Document: reduce Oversight; convert Salvage → Influence; improve/remove Paperwork
- Lobby (Politics): raise Standing with factions (changes perks + audit flavor)
- Requisition & Trade: mini-shop; buy/sell; buy Permits
- Intel & Forecast: reveal next Case’s enemy + site; pre-seal tiles / prep evidence
- Recovery & Containment: heal / reduce heat / temporary “liability suppression”

##### 12) Politics & Trading (Simple, Thematic)

- Oversight (persistent heat): higher levels make audits harsher/more frequent
- Faction Standing: small range bonuses that shape offers and audit patterns
- Permits (the “tradeable legality” item): consumables that legalize or soften actions (reduce Audit gain on next Relic, improve chain-of-custody, ignore a specific procedural constraint briefly, etc.)

### 2) Tri‑Council Cold War (Diplomacy Deckbuilder)
**Genre:** Political deckbuilder / crisis management  
**Pitch:** You play a small polity navigating the Kahlbrax/Cygnan/Aethelgard blocs. Cards are treaties, trade routes, espionage assets, propaganda, and Assembly votes. “Combat” is replaced by timed crises and influence contests.

**Signature twist**
- The Anchored Sequence offers “helpful” drafts that quietly lock you out of certain truths/endings.

### 3) Seed Sphere: Evolution Deckbuilder
**Genre:** Survival + evolution deckbuilder  
**Pitch:** Your deck is adaptations (symbioses, metabolisms, sensory organs, social structures). Encounters are environmental shocks + neighbor species pressure. Synergies are ecological (food-web combos, resilience stacks). No separate tech tree: your deck *is* your species.

**Signature twist**
- Wild Seer events tempt you with chaotic mutations: huge spikes in power with persistent, sometimes emergent drawbacks.

### 4) Axiom Current Casino (Balatro‑ish Harmonics)
**Genre:** Combo scoring / roguelite  
**Pitch:** You manipulate entanglement patterns. You build “hands” of harmonics (sets/sequences) from measurement cards; score is probability steered into outcomes (jumps, comms, relic unlocks). Pushing luck builds “Harmonic Deviations” heat.

**Signature twist**
- At heat thresholds, Intercessor intervention or reality glitches rewrite rules mid-run.
